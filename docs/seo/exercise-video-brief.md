# Exercise photo & video brief — top 10 exercise pages

**For:** Shivaliba (demonstrating) + whoever is filming.
**Why:** these 10 pages get the most Google impressions (90 days to 7 Oct 2026) but almost no clicks. Real footage of an HCPC-registered physio can earn a video thumbnail in Google results, and shows Google (and patients) a qualified person is behind the content.
**Script source:** every line to say is copied from the live page, so what is said on camera matches what is written. If anything is clinically wrong, change the page first, then film.

## Kit and setup (same for all 10)

- **Phone** is fine. Film landscape (16:9), 1080p, 30fps. Lock exposure/focus on the person.
- **Tripod** at hip height, 2–3 m away, whole body in frame with a little space above the head.
- **Plain wall** (white or light grey), clear floor, good daylight from the side or front. No windows behind.
- **Clothing:** fitted, plain, no logos — joints must be visible (knees/shoulders/neck as relevant). Matches the current illustrations: dark fitted top and leggings.
- **Sound:** lapel mic if possible; quiet room. Or film silent and add a voice-over afterwards.
- **No patients or other people in shot.** No home address details visible.

## Each video (aim for 45–75 seconds)

1. **Hook (3 s):** "How to do a [exercise name] — Shivaliba, HCPC-registered physiotherapist at PhysioOnClick."
2. **Set-up (side-on):** read the set-up line while getting into position.
3. **Two to three slow reps** (front-on, then side-on), saying each step as you do it.
4. **Cues:** say the form cues over a rep.
5. **Common mistake (5–8 s):** briefly *show* the first mistake, say why it's wrong, then correct it.
6. **Dose + safety (close):** say the dose and the safety line, then: "If you're not sure it's right for you, book an assessment at physioonclick.co.uk."

## Photos for each exercise (same shoot)

Take stills with the same framing, square crop in mind (subject centred):
- **Start position** and **end position** (side-on).
- **Mistake vs correct** pair (the first common mistake).
These can replace or sit alongside the current illustrations. Send originals (full resolution); resizing and watermarking happen on our side.

## After filming

1. Upload each video to the **PhysioOnClick YouTube channel** (public or unlisted-but-embeddable). Title: "[Exercise name] — how to do it properly | PhysioOnClick". Description: first line = the page link.
2. Send Claude, for each video: the YouTube link, the upload date, and the length. It goes into `lib/exercise-demo-videos.ts` — one line per exercise — and the page then shows the player and tells Google about the video.
3. Send the photo files; they go into the exercise image library.

## 1. Spanish Squat (Isometric)  
`/exercises/spanish-squat` · 689 Google impressions / 90 days

**Equipment:** A strong resistance band or strap, A solid anchor point such as a heavy table leg or a door anchor  
**Best angle:** side-on (shows knee/shin angle)

**Set-up (say):** Loop a strong band around the back of both knees and anchor the other end to something solid at about knee height in front of you. Step back until the band is tight, feet hip-width apart.

**Steps (say while doing):**
1. Let the band take some of your weight as you sit back into a squat.
2. Lower until your knees are bent to roughly a right angle, keeping your shins vertical and your chest up.
3. Hold the position still, with your weight even through both feet.
4. Stand back up to finish the hold.

**Cues:**
- Your shins stay vertical - the band lets you sit back without your knees travelling forward.
- The hold feels like steady, strong work in the front of the thighs.
- Any tendon pain stays low and does not build through the hold.

**Show this mistake, then correct it:** Letting the knees drift forward over the toes, which loads the tendon more.

**Close:** Hold 45s × 5 · once a day, 4 days a week · still hold. Stop and message your physio if the knee pain climbs during the hold or is worse the morning after.

## 2. Quad Sets  
`/exercises/quad-sets` · 196 Google impressions / 90 days

**Equipment:** A small rolled towel  
**Best angle:** side-on (shows knee/shin angle)

**Set-up (say):** Sit or lie with the operated leg straight out in front of you, with a small rolled towel under your ankle if that is comfortable.

**Steps (say while doing):**
1. Tighten the thigh muscle of the operated leg, pressing the back of the knee down toward the bed.
2. Feel the muscle firm up and the kneecap draw slightly up toward your hip.
3. Hold the squeeze while you keep breathing normally.
4. Relax the thigh fully and rest for a few seconds before the next one.

**Cues:**
- The knee stays straight the whole time - this is a squeeze, not a bend.
- You can feel the thigh muscle go hard if you rest a hand on it.
- Your breathing carries on steadily throughout the hold.

**Show this mistake, then correct it:** Holding your breath or bearing down while you squeeze.

**Close:** 10 reps, 10s hold · 3 times a day. Stop and message your physio if the knee becomes hot, red, and swollen, or the wound leaks fluid, or your calf becomes painful and swollen.

## 3. Wall Squat Hold  
`/exercises/wall-squat-hold` · 152 Google impressions / 90 days

**Equipment:** A clear stretch of smooth wall  
**Best angle:** side-on (shows knee/shin angle)

**Set-up (say):** Stand with your back against a smooth wall, feet about hip-width apart and a step or two out from the wall.

**Steps (say while doing):**
1. Slide your back down the wall until your knees are bent to a shallow, comfortable angle.
2. Start well short of a right angle - a small bend is enough during a flare-up.
3. Hold the position still, keeping your weight even through both feet.
4. Slide back up the wall to finish the hold.

**Cues:**
- The hold feels like steady, tolerable work in the front of your thighs.
- Your knees stay in line with your feet and do not drift inwards.
- Any tendon pain stays low and steady, not building through the hold.

**Show this mistake, then correct it:** Bending the knees so deep that the pain rises - less depth is fine early on.

**Close:** Hold 30s × 4 · once a day, 6 days a week · still hold. Stop and message your physio if the tendon pain climbs during the hold or is worse the morning after.

## 4. Wall Slide  
`/exercises/wall-slide` · 150 Google impressions / 90 days

**Equipment:** none  
**Best angle:** front-on and side-on

**Set-up (say):** Stand facing a wall, about a forearm's length away. Place the little-finger edge of both hands and forearms flat against the wall, elbows bent, roughly in a goalpost shape.

**Steps (say while doing):**
1. Keeping your forearms and hands in contact with the wall, slide them slowly upwards.
2. Go as high as you can while keeping contact and without pain or shrugging.
3. Slide slowly back down to the start, gently drawing your shoulder blades down as you lower.

**Cues:**
- Keep your ribs down and your lower back flat - do not arch to reach higher.
- Forearms stay on the wall the whole way up and down.
- Shoulders stay away from your ears.

**Show this mistake, then correct it:** Arching the back to gain height.

**Close:** 3 sets × 10 reps · once a day, 5 days a week · slow. Stop and message your physio if the shoulder pinches sharply or catches each time you lift.

## 5. Isometric Neck Hold  
`/exercises/isometric-neck-hold` · 97 Google impressions / 90 days

**Equipment:** none  
**Best angle:** front-on and side-on

**Set-up (say):** Sit tall on a firm chair with your head balanced comfortably over your shoulders and your eyes looking straight ahead.

**Steps (say while doing):**
1. Place the palm of one hand flat against the side of your head, just above your ear.
2. Gently press your head sideways into your hand, letting your hand hold your head completely still.
3. Build to a light, steady effort of about a quarter of your strength and hold it.
4. Slowly ease off and rest, then repeat pressing forwards into your hand, backwards, and to the other side.

**Cues:**
- Your head does not actually move - the effort is met and held by your hand.
- The push is gentle and steady, never a sudden shove.
- Your jaw, shoulders and breathing stay relaxed during the hold.

**Show this mistake, then correct it:** Pushing too hard, especially in the early weeks - this should feel easy and controlled.

**Close:** Hold 10s × 3 · once a day, 6 days a week. Stop and message your physio if it brings on dizziness, pins and needles in the arms or hands, or a severe or unusual headache.

## 6. Elbow Flexion/Extension  
`/exercises/elbow-flexion-extension` · 85 Google impressions / 90 days

**Equipment:** none  
**Best angle:** front-on and side-on

**Set-up (say):** Sit at a table with your upper arm resting on the table and your forearm and hand free to move over the edge, palm facing up. If that is awkward, rest the arm on a pillow on your lap.

**Steps (say while doing):**
1. Slowly bend your elbow, bringing your hand up towards your shoulder as far as it comfortably goes.
2. Pause for a moment at the end of the movement.
3. Slowly straighten the elbow as far as it comfortably goes.
4. Let the arm relax at each end rather than pushing hard against the stiffness.

**Cues:**
- Move only into a gentle stretch feeling, never into sharp pain.
- The bending and straightening are slow and smooth.
- Keep the rest of your arm and shoulder relaxed.

**Show this mistake, then correct it:** Forcing into a hard stretch, or letting someone crank the elbow for you.

**Close:** 10 reps · 4 times a day · slow and controlled. Stop and message your physio if the elbow becomes hot or swollen, feels like it is blocking, or gets noticeably stiffer over a few days.

## 7. Levator Scapulae Stretch  
`/exercises/levator-scapulae-stretch` · 74 Google impressions / 90 days

**Equipment:** none  
**Best angle:** front-on and side-on

**Set-up (say):** Sit tall on a firm chair. Hold the bottom of the seat with the hand on the side you want to stretch, to keep that shoulder down.

**Steps (say while doing):**
1. Turn your head about 45 degrees towards the opposite armpit, so you are looking down and away from the tight side.
2. Let your chin drop gently towards your chest until you feel a stretch behind the shoulder blade on the held side.
3. Rest your other hand lightly on the back of your head to add a little weight, without pulling.
4. Hold the stretch, then slowly lift your head and let go of the seat.

**Cues:**
- You are looking down towards your armpit, not straight down or straight to the side.
- The stretch is felt at the back and side of the neck, towards the shoulder blade.
- The shoulder being stretched stays down, anchored by your hand on the seat.

**Show this mistake, then correct it:** Pulling firmly on your head instead of letting its weight do the work.

**Close:** Hold 30s × 2 · twice a day · slow and controlled. Stop and message your physio if it brings on dizziness, pins and needles in the arms or hands, or a severe or unusual headache.

## 8. Ankle Pumps (Post-Surgery)  
`/exercises/ankle-pumps-post-surgery` · 65 Google impressions / 90 days

**Equipment:** none  
**Best angle:** side-on (shows knee/shin angle)

**Set-up (say):** Lie in bed or sit propped up with the legs out straight in front of you and the calves resting on the bed.

**Steps (say while doing):**
1. Point your toes and feet gently away from you, as if pressing a pedal.
2. Pull your toes and feet back up toward your face.
3. Move smoothly between the two positions, one ankle at a time or both together.
4. You can add slow ankle circles in each direction once pumping feels easy.

**Cues:**
- The movement comes from your ankles, with your knees staying still and relaxed.
- You feel your calf muscles working gently as your foot moves.
- Keep breathing steadily and stay relaxed through your hips and the surgical area.

**Show this mistake, then correct it:** Tensing your thigh or the operated area to force a bigger movement.

**Close:** 15 reps · 5 times a day. Stop and seek urgent medical advice if you get new calf pain, swelling, redness, or heat, or you become short of breath - these can be signs of a blood clot.

## 9. Hip Hitch (Pelvic Drop)  
`/exercises/hip-hitch` · 57 Google impressions / 90 days

**Equipment:** A step or the bottom stair, A wall or rail for light balance support  
**Best angle:** side-on (shows knee/shin angle)

**Set-up (say):** Stand sideways on a step with one foot, the other foot hanging free over the edge. Rest a hand on a wall or rail for balance only. Stand tall through the standing leg.

**Steps (say while doing):**
1. Keep the standing knee straight but not locked.
2. Let the free-side hip drop slowly towards the floor by a few centimetres.
3. Use the muscles on the side of the standing hip to lift that hip back up until the pelvis is level, or very slightly higher.
4. Move slowly and with control in both directions.

**Cues:**
- The movement comes from hitching the pelvis, not from bending or straightening the standing knee.
- You feel the work in the side of the standing buttock and hip.
- Your upper body stays tall and still - you are not leaning over.

**Show this mistake, then correct it:** Bending and straightening the standing knee to create the movement.

**Close:** 3 sets × 12 reps · once a day, 5 days a week · slow and controlled. Stop and message your physio if this brings on sharp outer-hip pain, or pain that lingers and disturbs your sleep that night.

## 10. Chin Tuck  
`/exercises/chin-tuck` · 55 Google impressions / 90 days

**Equipment:** none  
**Best angle:** front-on and side-on

**Set-up (say):** Sit or stand tall, looking straight ahead. Keep your face vertical and your eyes level.

**Steps (say while doing):**
1. Without tipping your head down, gently glide your chin straight backwards to make a light 'double chin'.
2. You should feel a mild stretch at the base of your skull and the back of your neck.
3. Hold briefly, then release forwards to the start - do not push the head forwards past neutral.

**Cues:**
- The movement is horizontal - like a drawer sliding back, not a nod.
- Your eyes and jaw stay level throughout.
- Gentle and small; you are not trying to press hard.

**Show this mistake, then correct it:** Nodding the chin down towards the chest instead of gliding it back.

**Close:** 10 reps · 5 times a day. Stop and message your physio if it brings on dizziness, pins and needles in the arms or hands, or a severe headache.
