## A. What we offer (about your own service)

1. **Please confirm these statements, which appear on several pages:**
   - We don't order scans ourselves.
   - Patients need a camera, a microphone and a stable internet connection.
   - We offer no in-person clinic or home visits anywhere.
   - We don't use needles (acupuncture or dry needling) or prescribe medicines.
2. **Session numbers and recovery times.** Pages say things like "most plans run 4–8 sessions across 6–10 weeks" and "post-surgery rehab runs longer". These come from the existing service pages and are presented as *our* plans. Do they reflect your practice?
3. **Health insurance.** We tell patients we can't promise any insurer will pay, and to check first. Do you know whether Bupa, AXA, Vitality or Aviva have accepted your invoices before, or have you registered as a recognised provider with any insurer? If yes, we can say so on the pages.
4. **Women's and pelvic health.** We currently don't offer or mention it. Do you want to offer it online? If so, it would be a future set of pages.

## B. Neurological conditions

5. **Are you comfortable with pages for stroke recovery, Parkinson's, MS and FND?** The pages say:
   - we support people who are medically stable;
   - we work alongside their NHS team (stroke team, neurologist, MS nurse), by video, with a carer welcome;
   - triage at booking confirms whether video suits them;
   - we claim no specialist status.
6. **MS relapses.** The NHS gives no relapse advice, so the page uses our own approach: "If none of the 999 signs apply and you think you're having a relapse, contact your MS team or GP. We can't assess a relapse over video, and we pause exercise with us until they've advised you." Is that right?
7. **FND.** The main trial of physiotherapy for functional motor disorder (Physio4FMD, Lancet Neurology 2024) **did not** meet its main outcome. The page says so honestly and makes no outcome claims. It also says a neurologist makes the diagnosis and that we ask for it before starting. Are you comfortable with that framing?
8. **Service page wording.** The existing neuro service page says "improved balance confidence and reduced fall-catches within a few weeks is common… gains building over 8–12 weeks". This has no published source, and the new pages say we promise no outcomes. Should we soften it (e.g. "we review your progress every few weeks") or keep it?

## C. After surgery

9. **Knee replacement, hip replacement and rotator cuff repair pages.** Each says:
   - online rehab starts once the surgical team has cleared exercise-based rehab;
   - wound checks and complications go back to the surgical team.

   Is that right for your practice? Is there anything you'd add?
10. **Warning signs after rotator cuff surgery.** We couldn't find an NHS page on shoulder-surgery complications, so we reused the NHS knee and hip replacement warning signs: clot or infection signs go to urgent GP or 111; chest pain or breathlessness goes to 999. Are these right for shoulder surgery?
11. **Rotator cuff recovery time.** Our service page says 4–6 months. The hospital leaflet we used (Southampton) says up to 9 months of exercises. Which should the page state, or should it just say "your surgeon will give you a timeline"?
12. **Post-surgery service wording.** "Patients who follow a progressive loading plan… report less residual stiffness and a faster return". This has no published source. Soften it or keep it?

## D. Warning signs (red flags)

Every page sends warning symptoms to care at least as urgent as the NHS advises. A few couldn't be matched to an NHS page, so we chose the safer option:

13. **Please confirm these choices:**
    - Heel that is hot and swollen, with a fever → NHS 111 or an urgent GP.
    - Tennis elbow with gradually increasing finger numbness → see your GP (sudden numbness or weakness → 999, as a possible stroke).
    - Neck pain after an accident, or with dizziness → 999 or A&E, or 111 if unsure.
    - Shoulder pain: hot or cold arm, no feeling in the arm, severe pain in both shoulders, or feeling feverish or unwell → urgent GP or 111.
14. **Anything missing?** Is there a warning sign you'd always want listed for any of these conditions that we haven't included?
