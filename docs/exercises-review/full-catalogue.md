## Spine & Neck (22)

#### ex-3 - Bridge Progression
_Back pain / Strength phase_ &nbsp; · &nbsp; summary (unchanged): Targets hip and trunk strength to improve movement tolerance.

- **Equipment:** Exercise mat
- **Get set up:** Lie on your back on a mat with your knees bent and your feet flat on the floor, hip-width apart. Rest your arms by your sides.
- **Steps:**
  1. Gently tighten your lower tummy and squeeze your buttocks.
  2. Lift your hips off the floor until your body makes a straight line from your shoulders to your knees.
  3. Hold for a moment, keeping your tummy gently engaged.
  4. Lower your hips back down slowly, one part of your spine at a time.
- **Good form:** Your hips stay level with each other, not dropping to one side. / The effort is felt in your buttocks and the backs of your thighs. / Your lower back stays long and comfortable, not pinched.
- **Ease off / stop if:** Lifting so high that your lower back arches and feels squeezed. / Pushing through your toes instead of through your whole foot. / Holding your breath — keep breathing steadily throughout. / Stop and message your physio if the pain spreads into your buttock or down your leg, or you notice new numbness, pins and needles, or weakness.
- **Starting dose:** 2 sets × 10 reps · once a day, 5 days a week

#### ex-13 - Chin Tuck
_Neck pain / Early rehab_ &nbsp; · &nbsp; summary (unchanged): Encourages gentle neck alignment through a small, comfortable tucking motion.

- **Equipment:** none
- **Get set up:** Sit or stand tall, looking straight ahead. Keep your face vertical and your eyes level.
- **Steps:**
  1. Without tipping your head down, gently glide your chin straight backwards to make a light 'double chin'.
  2. You should feel a mild stretch at the base of your skull and the back of your neck.
  3. Hold briefly, then release forwards to the start - do not push the head forwards past neutral.
- **Good form:** The movement is horizontal - like a drawer sliding back, not a nod. / Your eyes and jaw stay level throughout. / Gentle and small; you are not trying to press hard.
- **Ease off / stop if:** Nodding the chin down towards the chest instead of gliding it back. / Holding your breath or clenching your jaw. / Pushing into pain - this should feel like a gentle stretch at most. / Stop and message your physio if it brings on dizziness, pins and needles in the arms or hands, or a severe headache.
- **Starting dose:** 10 reps · 5 times a day - _Hold each tuck for about 5 seconds. Easy to fit in through the day at a desk._
- **Figure pose:** `chinTuck`

#### ex-14 - Dead Bug
_Low back pain / Strength phase_ &nbsp; · &nbsp; summary (unchanged): Builds core control by moving the arms and legs slowly while keeping the trunk steady.

- **Equipment:** Exercise mat
- **Get set up:** Lie on your back on a mat. Reach both arms straight up over your shoulders, and lift your feet so your hips and knees are bent at right angles.
- **Steps:**
  1. Gently flatten your lower back towards the floor by tightening your lower tummy.
  2. Slowly lower one arm back overhead and straighten the opposite leg towards the floor.
  3. Stop before your lower back starts to arch up away from the floor.
  4. Bring the arm and leg back to the start, then repeat with the other pair.
- **Good form:** Your lower back stays gently pressed towards the floor the whole time. / Only your arms and legs move — your hips and ribs stay still. / The movement is slow and controlled, not rushed.
- **Ease off / stop if:** Letting your lower back lift into an arch as the leg lowers. / Moving so fast that your trunk wobbles or twists. / Holding your breath — breathe out gently as your arm and leg move away. / Stop and message your physio if the pain spreads further down your leg, or you notice new numbness, pins and needles, or weakness.
- **Starting dose:** 2 sets × 8 reps · once a day, 5 days a week - _Count 1 repetition as one movement to each side._
- **Figure pose:** `(name-inferred)`

#### ex-15 - Bird Dog
_Back pain / Strength phase_ &nbsp; · &nbsp; summary (unchanged): Improves spinal stability by extending opposite arm and leg in a slow, controlled pattern.

- **Equipment:** Exercise mat
- **Get set up:** Kneel on all fours on a mat, with your hands under your shoulders and your knees under your hips. Keep your back flat and your head in line with your spine.
- **Steps:**
  1. Gently tighten your lower tummy to steady your back.
  2. Slowly reach one arm forwards while straightening the opposite leg out behind you.
  3. Stretch them out only until they are level with your body, no higher.
  4. Hold briefly, then lower back to all fours with control.
  5. Repeat with the other arm and leg.
- **Good form:** Your back stays flat and level, like a table top. / Your hips stay square to the floor and do not tip. / The reaching arm and leg stop at body height, not lifted above it.
- **Ease off / stop if:** Arching your lower back or letting your tummy sag towards the floor. / Twisting your hips or shoulders as you reach. / Rushing — each reach should be slow and steady. / Stop and message your physio if the pain spreads into your buttock or leg, or you notice new numbness, pins and needles, or weakness.
- **Starting dose:** 2 sets × 10 reps · once a day, 5 days a week - _Count 1 repetition as one reach to each side._

#### ex-17 - McKenzie Press-Up
_Lumbar disc-related back pain / Early rehab_ &nbsp; · &nbsp; summary (unchanged): Gentle repeated lower-back extension to centralise leg symptoms toward the spine, following the McKenzie extension principle.

- **Equipment:** Exercise mat
- **Get set up:** Lie face down on a mat with your hands flat on the floor under your shoulders, as if you were about to push up. Let your hips and legs stay relaxed.
- **Steps:**
  1. Push through your hands to lift your chest and upper body off the floor.
  2. Let your lower back and tummy sag towards the floor as you come up.
  3. Rise only as far as feels comfortable, keeping your hips on the mat.
  4. Lower back down slowly to the starting position.
- **Good form:** Your hips, buttocks and legs stay loose and resting on the mat. / Any symptoms in your leg move up towards your back, or stay the same — this is the direction you want. / The movement is a slow, repeated rock rather than a long held stretch at first.
- **Ease off / stop if:** Tensing your buttocks or legs as you lift. / Pushing up hard and fast into a big arch straight away — build the range up gradually. / Stop and message your physio if the pain spreads further down your leg, or you notice new or worse numbness, pins and needles, or weakness in your leg or foot.
- **Starting dose:** 1 set × 10 reps · 3 times a day · slow and controlled
- **Figure pose:** `(name-inferred)`

#### ex-19 - Cat-Cow Stretch
_Thoracic stiffness / Early rehab_ &nbsp; · &nbsp; summary (unchanged): Alternates gentle spinal flexion and extension on hands and knees to restore comfortable segmental movement.

- **Equipment:** Exercise mat
- **Get set up:** Start on your hands and knees on a mat, with your hands under your shoulders and your knees under your hips. Let your back settle into a flat, neutral position.
- **Steps:**
  1. Breathe out and gently round your back up towards the ceiling, letting your head drop and your tailbone tuck under.
  2. Breathe in and slowly reverse the movement, letting your tummy sink, your chest lift and your gaze rise a little.
  3. Move smoothly between the two positions, letting your breathing set the pace.
  4. Finish back in the flat, neutral starting position.
- **Good form:** The movement flows slowly and evenly, led by your breathing. / You move only as far as stays comfortable in each direction. / The bend is spread along your whole spine, not forced at one spot.
- **Ease off / stop if:** Pushing hard into the end of either position. / Rushing so the movement becomes a jerky flick. / Holding your breath instead of letting it guide the pace. / Stop and message your physio if it brings on pins and needles in your arms or hands, or pain that spreads rather than easing.
- **Starting dose:** 1 set × 10 reps · twice a day · slow and controlled - _Count 1 repetition as one round up and one arch down._
- **Figure pose:** `catCow`

#### ex-20 - Thoracic Rotation (Open Book)
_Thoracic stiffness / Mobility phase_ &nbsp; · &nbsp; summary (unchanged): Side-lying rotation opening the chest toward the ceiling, restoring rotation range often lost with prolonged sitting.

- **Equipment:** Exercise mat, A pillow for your head
- **Get set up:** Lie on your side on a mat with your knees drawn up towards your chest and a pillow under your head. Stretch both arms out in front of you at shoulder height, palms together.
- **Steps:**
  1. Keeping your knees together and still, slowly lift your top arm and open it out towards the ceiling.
  2. Follow your hand with your eyes and let your head and chest turn to follow it.
  3. Take the arm as far as it goes comfortably, aiming to lower it towards the floor on the other side.
  4. Hold briefly, then slowly bring the arm back over to meet the other hand.
- **Good form:** Your knees stay stacked and pressed together throughout. / The turn comes from your mid-back and chest, not from your lower back. / You feel a gentle opening across the front of your chest and through your mid-back.
- **Ease off / stop if:** Letting your top knee lift away so the movement comes from your hips. / Forcing the arm down to the floor before your back is ready. / Holding your breath - breathe out gently as you open. / Stop and message your physio if it brings on pins and needles in your arm or hand, or pain that spreads down your arm.
- **Starting dose:** 1 set × 8 reps · twice a day · slow, with a short hold at the end - _Work the stiffer side, or both sides if your physio advises. Count 1 repetition as one open and return._

#### ex-23 - Isometric Neck Hold
_Whiplash-associated disorder / Strength phase_ &nbsp; · &nbsp; summary (unchanged): Gentle resistance pushing the head into a supporting hand without movement, rebuilding neck muscle endurance safely.

- **Equipment:** none
- **Get set up:** Sit tall on a firm chair with your head balanced comfortably over your shoulders and your eyes looking straight ahead.
- **Steps:**
  1. Place the palm of one hand flat against the side of your head, just above your ear.
  2. Gently press your head sideways into your hand, letting your hand hold your head completely still.
  3. Build to a light, steady effort of about a quarter of your strength and hold it.
  4. Slowly ease off and rest, then repeat pressing forwards into your hand, backwards, and to the other side.
- **Good form:** Your head does not actually move - the effort is met and held by your hand. / The push is gentle and steady, never a sudden shove. / Your jaw, shoulders and breathing stay relaxed during the hold.
- **Ease off / stop if:** Pushing too hard, especially in the early weeks - this should feel easy and controlled. / Letting your head drift or poke forward as you press. / Holding your breath through the effort. / Stop and message your physio if it brings on dizziness, pins and needles in the arms or hands, or a severe or unusual headache.
- **Starting dose:** Hold 10s × 3 · once a day, 6 days a week - _Work four gentle directions each time: pressing left, right, forwards and backwards. Start with a short, light hold and build the time and effort up over several weeks._

#### ex-26 - Segmental Rolling
_Acute low back pain / Early rehab_ &nbsp; · &nbsp; summary (unchanged): Rolling from back to side in a controlled, segmental way to reintroduce comfortable movement after an acute flare.

- **Equipment:** Exercise mat
- **Get set up:** Lie on your back on a mat or a firm bed, with your legs straight and your arms by your sides.

  2. Reach that arm across your body and let your shoulder follow it.
  3. Let your ribs, then your hips, then your legs roll one after the other until you are on your side.

#### ex-151 - Eccentric Wrist Flexion
_Medial epicondylalgia (golfer's elbow) / Strength phase_ &nbsp; · &nbsp; summary: Slowly lowering a light weight through wrist flexion, the evidence-informed way to load the forearm flexor tendon on the inner elbow.

- **Body part / area:** Wrist / upper_limb

- **Equipment:** A light dumbbell, starting around 0.5 to 1 kg; A table or your thigh to rest the forearm on

- **Dose note:** Expect some inner-forearm ache during and after - this is a loading exercise and it should settle within a day. Add a small amount of weight only once 3 sets of 15 feel easy.

#### ex-166 - Spanish Squat (Isometric)
_Patellar tendinopathy (flare-up) / Early rehab_ &nbsp; · &nbsp; summary: A held squat with a strong band looped behind the knees and anchored in front, letting you load the quads hard with the shins kept vertical - a low-irritation way to settle tendon pain.

- **Body part / area:** Knee / lower_limb
- **Tags:** knee, isometric, tendon-loading, early-rehab
- **Equipment:** A strong resistance band or strap; A solid anchor point such as a heavy table leg or a door anchor
- **Get set up:** Loop a strong band around the back of both knees and anchor the other end to something solid at about knee height in front of you. Step back until the band is tight, feet hip-width apart.
- **Steps:**
  1. Let the band take some of your weight as you sit back into a squat.

#### ex-167 - Reverse Nordic (Quad Eccentric)

- **Dose note:** A demanding exercise - start with a very small backward range, or hold a pole in front for support, and build up slowly over weeks.
