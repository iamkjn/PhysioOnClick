## 1. Quick Source Scan

- NHS, "Physiotherapy". Page last reviewed 3 April 2025. Current NHS patient information. Relevant point: physiotherapy can include education, advice, movement, exercise, manual therapy and an assessment of symptoms, medical history and lifestyle. https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/physiotherapy/
- Chartered Society of Physiotherapy, "Remote consultations". Current professional guidance page, accessed 14 September 2026. Relevant point: remote physiotherapy can support triage, assessment, self-management information, rehabilitation and monitoring when appropriate. https://www.csp.org.uk/professional-clinical/professional-guidance/remote-consultations
- Chartered Society of Physiotherapy, "Telehealth". Last reviewed 7 July 2025. Professional body source. Relevant point: remote and digital physiotherapy should be clinically appropriate, safe and based on professional judgement. https://www.csp.org.uk/professional-clinical/clinical-innovation/digital-physiotherapy/telehealth
- HCPC, "Standards of proficiency - Physiotherapists". Effective from 1 September 2023. Regulator source. Relevant point: physiotherapists must gather appropriate information, undertake assessment, use evidence-informed practice and evaluate intervention plans. https://www.hcpc-uk.org/standards/standards-of-proficiency/physiotherapists/
- NHS, "Back pain". Current NHS patient information, accessed 14 September 2026. Relevant point: some symptoms with back pain, including bladder/bowel changes, numbness around genitals or bottom, chest pain, fever, trauma or severe worsening symptoms, need urgent assessment. https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/back-pain/
- ASA/CAP, "Health: Therapies (General)". Published 2 March 2022. UK advertising guidance. Relevant point: healthcare marketing claims must be substantiated, responsible and must not discourage essential medical treatment. https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/health-therapies-general.html
