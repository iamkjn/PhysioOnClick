## 1. Quick Source Scan

- NICE, "Joint replacement (primary): hip, knee and shoulder" NG157. Published 4 June 2020; last reviewed 19 December 2024. Evergreen. Relevant point: guidance covers support and rehabilitation before and after planned hip, knee and shoulder replacement. https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng157
- NHS, "Hip replacement". Page modified 21 May 2025. Evergreen. Relevant point: recovery can take several months, varies by person, and physiotherapists advise on recovery; early walking and home exercise advice are part of recovery. https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/hip-replacement/
- NHS, "Knee replacement - recovery". Page modified 31 July 2026; page last reviewed 9 March 2023. Genuinely recent page modification. Relevant point: physiotherapists help with walking and home exercise programmes; exercises support long-term strength and movement; swelling management and confidence with walking aids are included. https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/knee-replacement/recovery/
- NHS, "ACL surgery - recovering". Page last reviewed 17 July 2025; modified 28 July 2025. Evergreen. Relevant point: recovery varies, physiotherapy supports exercises and advice, and early exercises support long-term knee strength and movement. https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/acl-anterior-cruciate-ligament-surgery/recovering/
- NHS, "DVT (deep vein thrombosis)". Page last reviewed 30 April 2026; modified 11 August 2026. Genuinely recent update. Relevant safety point: urgent help is advised for suspected DVT; emergency help is advised if DVT symptoms occur with shortness of breath or chest pain. https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/deep-vein-thrombosis-dvt/

## 4. Facebook Pack

Post copy:  
"After surgery, progress is not just about pain levels.

A physiotherapy review usually looks at the whole recovery pattern:

- Pain: is it settling, stable, or escalating?
- Swelling: is it controlled, or increasing?
- Range of movement: is the joint moving more safely?
- Strength and control: are daily activities becoming easier?
- Confidence: are walking, stairs, sleep, work, sport or arm use becoming less worrying?

The right pace depends on the operation, surgeon guidance, symptoms and goals. Rehab should respond to how the body is coping rather than forcing a fixed timeline.

Seek urgent medical advice if you notice severe or worsening pain, increasing swelling/redness, wound discharge, fever, calf pain or swelling, chest pain, breathlessness, or new neurological symptoms.
