## 1. Quick Source Scan

- GOV.UK / Department of Health and Social Care, "UK Chief Medical Officers' physical activity guidelines". Updated 10 July 2026. Genuinely current UK public health source. Relevant point: adults are advised to build regular activity across the week, with strengthening activity included; people should adapt activity to ability and circumstances. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/physical-activity-guidelines-uk-chief-medical-officers-report
- World Physiotherapy, "World PT Day toolkit 2026 now available". Published 8 June 2026. Genuinely current campaign source. Relevant point: World PT Day 2026 focuses on cardiovascular disease and stroke, including physiotherapy's role in prevention, management and rehabilitation. https://world.physio/news/world-pt-day-toolkit-2026-now-available
- World Physiotherapy, "World PT Day 2026 information sheets". Current 2026 campaign resource. Relevant point: campaign materials highlight physical activity as part of reducing cardiovascular and stroke risk, while positioning physiotherapy as support for safe, appropriate movement. https://world.physio/toolkit/world-pt-day-2026-information-sheets
- NHS, "Stroke - Causes". Last reviewed 13 August 2025. Current NHS patient information. Relevant point: risk factors for stroke include high blood pressure, high cholesterol, diabetes, smoking, excess alcohol, obesity and inactivity; healthy lifestyle changes can reduce risk. https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/stroke/causes/
- NHS, "Cardiovascular disease". Last reviewed 26 January 2026. Current NHS patient information. Relevant point: cardiovascular disease affects the heart or blood vessels and prevention includes healthy eating, regular exercise, stopping smoking and reducing alcohol intake. https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cardiovascular-disease/
- NICE, "Cardiovascular disease: risk assessment and reduction, including lipid modification" NG238. Last reviewed 2 September 2025. Current UK clinical guideline. Relevant point: cardiovascular risk reduction should be evidence-based, personalised and linked to a person's overall risk profile. https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng238
