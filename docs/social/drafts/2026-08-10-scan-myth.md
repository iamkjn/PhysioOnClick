## 1. Quick Source Scan

- NICE, "Low back pain and sciatica in over 16s: assessment and management" NG59. Published 30 November 2016; last reviewed 29 July 2026. Evergreen but freshly reviewed. Key point: imaging is not routinely offered in non-specialist settings for low back pain with or without sciatica, and specialist imaging is considered when results are likely to change management. https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng59
- NICE recommendations, imaging section. Recommendation wording from 2016 remains active in the current guideline. Evergreen. https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/NG59/chapter/Recommendations#imaging
- NHS, "Back pain". Page last reviewed 5 March 2026; next review due 5 March 2029. Current NHS patient advice says back pain is common, often improves within weeks, staying active can help, and lists urgent red flags such as sudden/worsening severe pain, fever/unwell symptoms, bilateral neurological symptoms, bladder/bowel changes, chest pain, or serious accident. https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/back-pain/
- Chartered Society of Physiotherapy, "Video exercises for back pain". Last reviewed 27 May 2020; last revised 29 August 2024. Evergreen safety point: seek advice from a physiotherapist or healthcare professional before starting a new exercise regime, and seek urgent medical advice if exercises worsen pain. https://www.csp.org.uk/conditions/back-pain/video-exercises-back-pain
