## 1. Quick Source Scan

- NHS, "Physiotherapy". Page last reviewed 3 April 2025. Current NHS patient information. Relevant point: a first physiotherapy appointment includes questions about symptoms, medical history and lifestyle, and may include checks such as strength and balance before discussing treatment options. https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/physiotherapy/
- Chartered Society of Physiotherapy, "Digital, print, virtual and telephysiotherapy". Last reviewed 29 June 2026. Genuinely recent professional guidance. Relevant point: personalised physiotherapy advice should be based on proper assessment, and the physiotherapist must decide whether in-person care is needed for safe care. https://www.csp.org.uk/professional-clinical/professional-guidance/insurance/digital-virtual-physiotherapy
- Chartered Society of Physiotherapy, "Remote consultations". Current professional guidance page, accessed 9 September 2026. Relevant point: remote physiotherapy can be used for triage, assessment, self-management information, e-rehabilitation and monitoring, but delivery should be evaluated and appropriate for purpose. https://www.csp.org.uk/professional-clinical/professional-guidance/remote-consultations
- NHS England Digital, "Using video conferencing and consultation tools guidance for health and care professionals". Last edited 1 July 2026. Genuinely recent NHS guidance. Relevant point: video consultations should protect privacy, avoid inadvertent disclosure and document relevant outcomes like face-to-face consultations. https://digital.nhs.uk/data-and-information/information-governance/guidance/using-video-conferencing-and-consultation-tools/guidance-for-health-and-care-professionals
- HCPC, "Standards of proficiency - Physiotherapists". Effective from 1 September 2023. Regulator source. Relevant point: physiotherapists must understand assessment and intervention approaches, evaluate intervention plans and use evidence-informed practice. https://www.hcpc-uk.org/standards/standards-of-proficiency/physiotherapists/
- NICE, "Shared decision making" NG197. Published 17 June 2021. Evergreen UK healthcare guidance. Relevant point: healthcare decisions should involve clear discussion of options, risks, benefits and consequences. https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng197
