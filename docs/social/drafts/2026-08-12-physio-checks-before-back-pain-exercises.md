## 1. Quick Source Scan

- NICE, "Low back pain and sciatica in over 16s: assessment and management" NG59. Published 30 November 2016; last updated and last reviewed 29 July 2026. Genuinely recent review. Relevant points: assess for alternative diagnoses, use risk stratification at first contact where appropriate, tailor self-management advice, and take needs, preferences and capabilities into account when choosing exercise. https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng59
- NICE NG59 recommendations, assessment and non-invasive treatment sections. Evergreen active guidance. Relevant points: exclude specific causes such as cancer, infection, trauma or inflammatory disease when clinically suspected; consider simpler support such as reassurance, staying active and self-management for lower-risk cases; consider more complex support for higher-risk cases. https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/NG59/chapter/Recommendations
- NHS, "Back pain". Page last reviewed 5 March 2026; next review due 5 March 2029. Current patient advice says back pain is common, often improves within weeks, staying active can help, and exercises should be stopped if pain worsens. It lists urgent red flags including severe sudden/worsening pain, fever/unwell symptoms, both-leg numbness/weakness, bladder/bowel changes, chest pain, or serious accident. https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/back-pain/
- Chartered Society of Physiotherapy, "Video exercises for back pain". Published 27 May 2020; last revised 29 August 2024. Evergreen safety advice: seek advice from a physiotherapist or healthcare professional before starting a new exercise regime, and seek urgent medical advice if exercises worsen pain. https://www.csp.org.uk/conditions/back-pain/video-exercises-back-pain
